Why can rewards exceed the jackpot?

They are different pools. Key rewards are paid from feeds as they happen. The jackpot is the last feeder’s share of the remaining round pot. Available game SOL can also include earlier rounds.

Example: one 1 SOL feed in Whale mode, with no existing pot:

Key reward pool
0.30 SOL
Remaining round pot
0.50 SOL
Other allocations
0.20 SOL
Last feeder’s jackpot: 48% of 0.50
0.24 SOL

If you own every key, the 0.30 SOL key reward is yours—even though the jackpot is 0.24 SOL. That reward returns part of the SOL paid in; it is not 0.30 SOL profit. This illustration excludes an opening pot and rounding.

In the Whale example, the other 70% funds the pot, token holders and reserves. Your share is 10 of 11 keys—not 90.9% of their whole payment. No new buys means no new buy-funded rewards.

The last buyer wins the jackpot.

In this example, the other buyer wins because they bought last. Your earned SOL is still yours to claim.

Keys also share the closing key allocation. Keys reset next round; unclaimed SOL stays available.

New buyers move the price up.

As more SOL funds a round, its pricing curve makes keys more expensive. Earlier buyers can get more keys for the same SOL.
